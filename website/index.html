§ 01How it works

Three steps. No account, no upload.

Guidy sits in your menu bar until you need it. It works in any app that accepts a paste.

⌥ OptionSpace
hold, toggle or push-to-talk
01 · Shortcut

Press your shortcut

Hold it while you talk, or tap once to start and again to stop. Esc cancels. Pick any key combination you like.

02 · Speak

Talk normally

Voice detection trims the silence, then a model on your machine turns the rest into text. Streaming models show it as you go.

03 · Paste

The text lands at your cursor

Guidy pastes into whatever app has focus: your editor, a browser, a chat window or the terminal.

§ 02Overlay

See the words while you're still talking.

A small glass panel floats over every app while Guidy listens. With a streaming model it shows the transcript live. It opens on the monitor your cursor is on, at the top or bottom of the screen.

§ 04Meeting notes

Meeting notes, with no bot in the call.

Guidy records your microphone and your Mac's audio, so it works with any meeting app. After the call it writes a transcript with named speakers, a summary and action items. macOS 14.2+

01
Any meeting appNothing joins the call and there is no browser extension. Guidy spots meetings on your macOS Calendar and asks before it records.
02
Speakers by nameName a speaker once and Guidy recognizes their voice in later meetings. Voiceprints are kept only for people you name.
03
Summaries from a local modelUse the built-in llama.cpp model or Apple Intelligence. If you prefer a cloud model, bring your own API key.
04
Action items to LinearGuidy drafts tickets from the action items. Nothing is created until you approve it.
05
Encrypted on diskMeetings and their audio are encrypted with a key kept in the macOS Keychain. Raw audio is deleted after 3 days unless you choose otherwise.

§ 05Details

The small things, done properly.

Optional polishing

Run the transcript through an LLM with your own prompts: Apple Intelligence, a local model, or any provider with your key. It has its own shortcut and is off by default.

History

Every transcription is saved on your machine with its audio. Replay it, copy it, star it, or transcribe it again.

Your own models

Drop GGUF files into the models folder and they show up next to the catalog models.

26 interface languages

The app is translated into 26 languages, right-to-left included. Transcription language is auto-detected or set per model.

Scriptable

guidy --toggle-transcription starts and stops recording from a launcher, a Stream Deck or a script.

Quiet when it should be

Short start and stop sounds (marimba or pop), and an option to mute other audio while you record.

§ 08FAQ

Questions

Does any of my audio go to the cloud?
No. Recording and transcription run on your machine. Text only leaves your machine if you turn on post-processing or meeting summaries with a cloud provider and enter your own API key.
Which apps does it work with?
Any app that accepts a paste. On macOS Guidy needs Microphone access to hear you and Accessibility access to paste for you; it asks for both on first launch.
How much space do the models need?
The recommended models range from 218 MB (Canary 180M Flash) to about 1.8 GB (Cohere Transcribe). You only download the ones you pick, and you can delete them from the Models page.
Where is my data stored?
In the app data folder, ~/Library/Application Support/dev.guidy.app on macOS. API keys go in the macOS Keychain.
Can I use my own model?
Yes. Put a GGUF speech model in the models folder and it appears on the Models page as a custom model.
Is Guidy related to Handy?
Yes. Guidy's backend (audio, transcription, settings, history) comes from Handy by CJ Pais, under the MIT license. The interface, meeting notes and artwork are Guidy's own, written in Rust on GPUI.
